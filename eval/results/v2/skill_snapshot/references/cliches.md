# Tells and defaults

Read the sections that match the piece. These are reference points, not a blacklist: a listed move is fine when the brief truly calls for it and you can say why. Removing a tell only helps if what replaces it is better.

## Text tells (any kind of writing)

- Openers that warm up instead of starting: "In a world where", "Imagine a", "Have you ever", "In today's fast-paced", "Picture this".
- Inflated words standing in for content: tapestry, testament, delve, realm, embark, journey (for anything that is not travel), unlock, elevate, seamless, vibrant, bustling, intricate, beacon, symphony, dance (for anything that is not dancing).
- "It's not just X, it's Y" and other mirrored pairs built to sound like a slogan.
- Things in threes by habit: three adjectives, three examples, three short fragments in a row.
- Feelings named outright ("she felt a wave of sadness") where a detail would show them.
- Sensory roll call: one sight, one sound, one smell, in that order.
- A last line that sums up, lifts the mood, or turns the piece into a lesson.
- A question to the reader that only builds hype.
- Even rhythm: every sentence the same medium length.
- Unasked-for headers, bold labels, bullet lists and emoji.
- Hedged generalities that fit any subject: "more than ever", "at the end of the day", "the power of", "a world of possibilities".

## Story defaults

- Lonely figure looks at a vast view (Earth from orbit, the sea, the city at night) and realizes something about connection.
- Opening on weather or waking up.
- The twist: it was a dream, a simulation, a ghost, the narrator was dead, it was all on Earth.
- The protagonist talks to an AI, a photo, a plant or a recording to show loneliness.
- Stock names models reach for: Elara, Kael, Lyra, Seraphina, Thorne, Aria, Silas, Finn, Sarah Chen, Dr. Aris, Mira, Juniper.
- Quirky detective who notices tiny details; grizzled detective with a drinking problem; detective with an eidetic memory.
- An old shop, a mysterious stranger, a letter found in a drawer, a clock that stopped.
- Ending on a single small hopeful image: a sunrise, a smile, a flower in a crack.

## Poetry defaults

- Rhymed quatrains with moon/June, heart/apart, soul/whole, light/night.
- Abstract nouns doing the work: hope, time, love, silence, memory, dreams.
- Address to the subject ("O ocean") or a closing stanza that states the theme.
- Whispering winds, dancing leaves, golden sun, silver moon, gentle rain.

## Marketing and copy defaults

- "Say goodbye to X", "Meet the new", "Your new favorite", "Experience the difference", "Designed with you in mind", "Game-changer", "Revolutionize", "Next level".
- Benefit triads: faster, smarter, easier.
- A rhetorical problem question as the first line, followed by "Introducing...".
- Exclamation marks and a call to action as the last line.
- Promising feelings ("peace of mind", "confidence") instead of showing a moment of use.

## Naming defaults

- Two obvious words fused: CleanMyX, SwiftClean, MacSweep, TaskFlow.
- Suffix kits: -ify, -ly, -io, -hub, -verse, -sphere, -genie, -wise.
- Latin and Greek defaults: Lumina, Nova, Vita, Aether, Zenith, Apex, Nexus, Veritas.
- Mythology defaults: Atlas, Phoenix, Athena, Hermes, Orion.
- A name that only describes what the product does.

## Brainstorm and concept defaults

- "Uber for X", "AI-powered X", a marketplace, a subscription box, a community app.
- Ideas for the market's most visible member, not its underserved one.
- Five ideas that are one idea in five sizes.
- Purpose-washing: every idea "connects people" or "empowers creators".

## Positioning defaults

- "For people who care about quality", "the smart choice", "premium but accessible".
- Claiming the category's universal value (fast, simple, secure) as if it were unique.
- A tagline that could swap brands with a competitor.

## Second-order tells: what models do when told to "be creative"

These are the defaults of trying to avoid defaults. Treat them as defaults.

- The object, animal or building narrates.
- The piece is a log, diary, letter, receipt, error message, chat transcript or set of rules, chosen as a container rather than for meaning.
- Precise times and numbers sprinkled to look concrete ("At 3:47 a.m.", "the 14th cup").
- A dry footnote, parenthetical joke or small print gag as the ending.
- Second person present tense fragments by reflex.
- A villain who is secretly sympathetic; a hero who is secretly the villain.
- The quiet magic rule in an otherwise ordinary town; the elderly craftsman with an odd object.
- Lists of strange, specific sensory details that do not add up to a scene.
- Ending on one very short sentence, alone on its line.
- Weird for its own sake: surreal images that cannot be traced back to the brief.
